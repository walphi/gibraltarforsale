# Property to Buy in Gibraltar: Step-by-Step Purchase Process 2027

The Gibraltar conveyancing process shares DNA with England & Wales but has distinct local requirements — particularly around leasehold Crown land, service charge structures and registration. This guide breaks down each stage with practical timelines, decision points and common delays.

## Phase 1: Preparation (Weeks 1–2)

### Select & Instruct a Gibraltar Solicitor

**Criteria for selection**:
- Gibraltar-qualified (admitted to the Gibraltar Bar)
- Property department with 3+ conveyancers
- Professional Indemnity Insurance ≥ £2M
- Fixed-fee quote including searches, Land Registry, stamp duty filing
- Communication protocol (email/portal response time SLA)

**Recommended firms (alphabetical)**: Hassans, ISOLAS, Marrache & Co, Triay & Triay, Charles Gomez & Co.

**Instruction pack to provide**:
- [ ] Passport + proof of address (certified copies)
- [ ] Source of funds evidence (bank statements, gift letters, sale completion statements)
- [ ] Mortgage Agreement in Principle
- [ ] Property details (address, agent, price)
- [ ] Completed KYC/AML questionnaire

**Typical cost**: £1,800–£3,500 + VAT + disbursements (searches £400–£800, Land Registry £100–£500, Stamp Duty per scale)

### Mortgage Application (if applicable)

**Submit full application immediately** upon instruction. Typical timeline: 3–4 weeks to formal offer.

**Documents typically required**:
- 3 months bank statements (all accounts)
- 3 months payslips / 2–3 years SA302s (self-employed)
- P60 or tax overview
- Proof of deposit (savings statements, gift letter + donor ID)
- Credit report (UK/International)

**Gibraltar-specific**: Lenders instruct their own valuation (£300–£600). You pay. Valuation is for lending purposes only — **not a survey**.

## Phase 2: Pre-Exchange Due Diligence (Weeks 2–8)

### Week 2–3: Searches & Sales Pack Review

**Searches ordered by your solicitor**:
| Search | Purpose | Typical Turnaround |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| Land Registry title | Ownership, charges, restrictions, cautions | 3–5 days |
| Lease & variations | Term, ground rent, covenants, alienation provisions | 5–10 days |
| Service charge accounts (3 yrs) | Trends, arrears, sinking fund, major works planned | 5–10 days |
| Management company accounts | Financial health, litigation, insurance | 5–10 days |
| Planning / Building Control | Permissions, enforcement, completion certificates | 10–15 days |
| Local authority (LPS) | Road adoption, drainage, environmental | 10–15 days |
| Chancel / manorial rights | Rare but checked on older Town Area properties | 5 days |

**Sales pack from seller's solicitor** should arrive Week 2–3. Your solicitor reviews and raises **pre-contract enquiries** (typically 20–40 questions).

### Week 3–5: Survey & Enquiries

**Commission your survey** (Week 3 at latest):
- **Apartment**: RICS Level 2 (HomeBuyer Report) — £500–£800
- **House / Villa / Older property**: RICS Level 3 (Building Survey) — £800–£1,500
- **Specific concerns**: Drainage CCTV, roof drone inspection, cladding assessment

**Enquiry cycle**: Seller's solicitor responds (2–3 weeks). Your solicitor reviews responses and may raise **supplementary enquiries**. This ping-pong is the #1 cause of delay.

**Common enquiry topics**:
- Service charge disputes / litigation
- Unauthorised alterations (balcony enclosures, loft conversions)
- Leasehold consent requirements (subletting, alterations, pets)
- Parking allocation (deeded vs licence)
- Boundary discrepancies
- Asbestos / cladding / fire safety remediation status

### Week 5–6: Mortgage Offer & Final Review

**Mortgage offer received** — check:
- [ ] Loan amount matches requirement
- [ ] Valuation figure ≥ purchase price
- [ ] Conditions precedent (e.g., "subject to satisfactory retention for damp")
- [ ] Offer expiry date (typically 3–6 months)
- [ ] Product terms (rate, fees, ERCs, portability)

**Your solicitor provides Report on Title** — read it. It summarises:
- Title quality & any defects
- Lease terms & obligations
- Service charge position
- Planning / building control status
- Enquiry responses & outstanding issues
- Recommendations (retentions, indemnities, further investigations)

**Decision point**: Proceed, renegotiate, or withdraw.

## Phase 3: Exchange of Contracts (Week 8–10)

### Pre-Exchange Checklist

- [ ] Mortgage offer in hand (conditions satisfied or undertakings given)
- [ ] All enquiries satisfied or indemnity policies arranged
- [ ] Survey satisfactory or retention agreed
- [ ] Deposit funds (10%) cleared in solicitor's client account
- [ ] Buildings insurance **on risk from exchange** (your solicitor confirms)
- [ ] Completion date agreed (typically 2–4 weeks post-exchange)
- [ ] Leasehold consents obtained (freeholder licence to assign, mortgagee consent)

### Exchange Mechanics

1. **Contracts signed** by both parties (counterparts)
2. **Deposit transferred** to seller's solicitor (held as stakeholder or agent)
3. **Contracts dated** — exchange is legally binding from this moment
4. **Completion date fixed** — failure to complete = breach (deposit forfeiture, damages)

**After exchange**: You are contractually bound. Seller cannot accept other offers. You must complete.

## Phase 4: Between Exchange & Completion (Week 10–12)

### Buyer's Solicitor Actions

- [ ] **Pre-completion searches** (Land Registry priority search, bankruptcy search)
- [ ] **Request completion statement** from seller's solicitor (balance due)
- [ ] **Draw down mortgage funds** (lender requires 3–5 working days' notice)
- [ ] **Prepare transfer deed (TR1 equivalent)** for execution
- [ ] **Stamp Duty Land Tax return** drafted (filed post-completion)

### Buyer Actions

- [ ] Arrange **removals** for completion day
- [ ] **Utility transfers** — notify AquaGib, GIBTEL, gas (if applicable)
- [ ] **Service charge apportionment** — confirm with management agent
- [ ] **Key collection logistics** — confirm with agent (usually 12–2pm)

## Phase 5: Completion Day

### Timeline

| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| 09:00–10:00 | Buyer's solicitor sends completion funds (CHAPS) |
| 10:00–11:30 | Seller's solicitor confirms receipt |
| 11:30–12:00 | Key release authorised |
| 12:00–14:00 | Keys collected from agent |
| 14:00+ | Buyer takes possession |

**Funds must arrive by ~1:00pm** for same-day completion. Late arrival = next working day completion + daily interest penalty (typically 4% above base rate).

## Phase 6: Post-Completion (Week 12+)

### Solicitor Handles (Within 30 Days)

- [ ] **Stamp Duty return & payment** to Gibraltar Revenue Authority
- [ ] **Land Registry application** (Form FR1 equivalent) — registration 2–8 weeks
- [ ] **Notify management agent** of change of ownership
- [ ] **Provide you** with registered title, completion statement, invoice

### You Handle

- [ ] **Council tax / rates** registration (LPS)
- [ ] **Utility accounts** in your name
- [ ] **Service charge direct debit** to management agent
- [ ] **Buildings insurance** policy in your name (from exchange)
- [ ] **Update address** with bank, employer, DVLA, GP, electoral roll

## Common Delays & How to Mitigate

| Delay Cause | Typical Impact | Mitigation |
|-------------|----------------|------------|
| Leasehold consent (freeholder) | 4–8 weeks | Request consent **pre-exchange**; chase weekly |
| Management pack delays | 2–3 weeks | Solicitor to request directly; escalate to OFT if needed |
| Survey down-valuation | 1–2 weeks | Negotiate retention; appeal with comparable evidence |
| Mortgage offer expiry | Forces re-application | Align exchange/completion with offer validity |
| Chain dependencies | Unpredictable | Avoid chains; negotiate long-stop dates |
| Title defects (missing deeds, boundaries) | Weeks to months | Title indemnity insurance; negotiate price reduction |

## Leasehold-Specific Exchange/Completion Steps

If the property is **Crown leasehold** (common in Town Area, Upper Town, parts of South District):

1. **Licence to Assign** — Freeholder (Crown Estate / LPS) must consent to transfer
2. **Mortgagee Consent** — If existing charge, freeholder consent to new charge
3. **Deed of Covenant** — Buyer enters direct covenant with freeholder
4. **Registration of Assignment** — Post-completion, at Land Registry & freeholder's register

**Budget 4–8 weeks for freeholder consent**. Do not exchange without it unless contract conditions protect you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I exchange and complete same day?
Possible but risky. Requires all funds, searches, consents ready. Most solicitors advise 2+ weeks between.

### What if the seller doesn't complete?
Buyer can: (a) serve notice to complete (10 working days), (b) claim deposit + damages, (c) seek specific performance. Solicitor manages.

### Do I need a Gibraltar bank account?
Not for completion (CHAPS from UK/EU works). Helpful for service charges, utilities, mortgage payments.

### What if I'm buying with a mortgage from a non-Gibraltar lender?
Many UK lenders don't lend on Gibraltar property. Use Gibraltar-based lenders or international private banks.

### How long does Land Registry take?
2–8 weeks typically. Priority search protects you in the interim.

## Conclusion

The Gibraltar purchase process is **manageable but unforgiving of shortcuts**. The critical path is: **solicitor instruction → searches → enquiries → survey → mortgage offer → exchange**. Each stage depends on the previous. Invest in a communicative solicitor, respond to requests same-day, and build buffer for leasehold consents.

*This guide is general information. Always instruct a Gibraltar-qualified solicitor for your specific transaction.*
