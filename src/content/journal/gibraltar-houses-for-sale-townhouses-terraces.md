# Gibraltar Houses for Sale: Townhouses, Terraces & Detached Homes 2027

Houses represent a minority of Gibraltar's housing stock — approximately 15% of dwellings — but they attract disproportionate buyer interest for their space, privacy and renovation potential. This guide covers the three main house types, where to find them, pricing dynamics and the specific due diligence each requires.

## Gibraltar House Typology

| Type | Estimated Stock | Typical Areas | Price Range (2026 ask) |
|------|----------------|---------------|------------------------|
| **Detached Villas** | ~150 | South District, Europa Point, Eastside | £1.1M–£4M+ |
| **Semi-Detached / Terraced Houses** | ~400 | South District, Upper Town, Town Area, Westside | £500K–£1.5M |
| **Townhouses (3+ storeys)** | ~250 | Upper Town, Town Area, South District | £500K–£1.2M |

**Key distinction**: Unlike the UK, "house" in Gibraltar often means a **multi-storey townhouse on a small footprint** rather than a detached suburban home with garden. True detached properties with meaningful land are rare and command a significant premium.

## 1. Townhouses (Upper Town, Town Area, South District)

### Typical Characteristics
- **Footprint**: 50–90 m² per floor × 3–4 floors
- **Layout**: Kitchen/dining ground, living 1st, bedrooms 2nd/3rd, roof terrace top
- **Age**: 19th century garrison conversions to 1980s infill
- **Tenure**: Predominantly **Crown leasehold** (99/150-year terms from 1800s/1900s)

### Where to Find Them

| Sub-Area | Stock | Typical Price | Notes |
|----------|-------|---------------|-------|
| **Upper Town (Cannon Lane, Castle Road, Willis's Road)** | High | £500K–£1.1M | Character, steep access, parking challenges |
| **Town Area (Irish Town, Crutchett's Ramp, Library Ramp)** | Medium | £550K–£1.2M | Central, commercial neighbours, noise |
| **South District (Rosia Dale, Camp Bay Road)** | Low | £750K–£1.5M | Larger plots, better parking, quieter |

### Due Diligence Specific to Townhouses

1. **Leasehold term** — **Critical**. Many Upper Town leases originated 1850–1920. Unexpired term often 50–80 years.
   - Below 70 years: mortgage difficult, value depressed, extension costly
   - Check: Ground rent amount, review mechanism, freeholder (Crown Estate / LPS)

2. **Structural integrity** — Tall, narrow buildings on sloped ground
   - Party wall condition (shared with neighbours both sides)
   - Foundation movement on sloped sites (retaining walls, underpinning history)
   - Roof structure — original timber vs modern replacement

3. **Fire safety** — 3+ storeys, single staircase common
   - Check: Fire doors, escape routes, mains-powered alarms
   - Building Regulations compliance for any loft conversion

4. **Access & logistics** — Steep steps, narrow streets
   - Furniture delivery / removal access
   - Emergency vehicle access
   - Refuse collection point

5. **Party Wall Act obligations** — Any work affecting shared walls requires notice and surveyor appointment

### Renovation Potential & Value-Add

| Works | Typical Cost | Value Uplift | Consents Needed |
|-------|--------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Loft conversion (dormer/velux) | £40K–£70K | +15–20% | Planning + Building Control + Freeholder |
| Rear extension (ground/1st floor) | £60K–£100K | +10–15% | Planning + Building Control + Freeholder |
| Full refurbishment (shell) | £150K–£300K | +25–35% | Building Control; Planning if external changes |
| Roof terrace creation | £25K–£45K | +5–10% | Planning + Freeholder |

**Upper Town renovation buyers**: Budget 6–12 months for Planning + freeholder consent before works start.

## 2. Terraced / Semi-Detached Houses (South District, Westside, Town Area)

### Typical Characteristics
- **Footprint**: 70–130 m² per floor × 2–3 floors
- **Layout**: More generous than townhouses; often side return, small garden/yard
- **Age**: 1950s–1990s local authority / MoD sales; some 2000s infill
- **Tenure**: Mix of **freehold**, **long leasehold** (>100 yrs), and **Crown leasehold**

### Where to Find Them

| Sub-Area | Stock | Typical Price | Notes |
|----------|-------|---------------|-------|
| **South District (Rosia, Camp Bay, Little Bay fringes)** | Medium | £650K–£1.4M | Best gardens, parking, family demand |
| **Westside (Devil's Tower Road, Reclamation Road)** | Low | £600K–£1.2M | Modern, parking, near border |
| **Town Area (Crutchett's Ramp, Hospital Ramp)** | Low | £550K–£1.1M | Central, limited outdoor space |

### Due Diligence Specific to Terraces

1. **Boundary definition** — Party walls, fence lines, shared drains
   - Obtain Land Registry title plan + physical boundary walk
   - Check for encroachments (extensions, conservatories)

2. **Shared services** — Drains, soil stacks, sometimes water mains
   - Responsibility for repairs: check deeds / lease
   - CCTV drainage survey recommended pre-purchase

3. **Parking** — Critical for resale
   - Deeded space vs licence vs street permit
   - Verify on title plan; test access

4. **Leasehold vs Freehold** — If leasehold:
   - Unexpired term (target >100 years)
   - Ground rent (peppercorn vs escalating)
   - Service charge (if estate management)

5. **Noise & privacy** — Terraced = shared walls both sides
   - Sound insulation between units (test at viewing)
   - Garden/yard overlooked?

## 3. Detached Villas (South District, Europa Point, Eastside)

### Typical Characteristics
- **Plot**: 400–1,500+ m²
- **Footprint**: 200–400+ m² over 2–3 floors
- **Features**: Pool, garage (2+ cars), garden, sea views (many)
- **Age**: 1970s–2000s originals; 2010+ replacements
- **Tenure**: **Freehold** (rare) or **long Crown leasehold** (999-year or 150-year with modern terms)

### Where to Find Them

| Sub-Area | Stock | Typical Price | Notes |
|----------|-------|---------------|-------|
| **South District (Rosia, Camp Bay, Little Bay)** | ~80 | £1.1M–£2.8M | Established gardens, mature trees, quiet |
| **Europa Point (Lighthouse, Europa Point Road)** | ~40 | £1.3M–£3.5M | Newer builds, modern spec, panoramic views |
| **Eastside (Catalan Bay cliffs, Sandy Bay)** | ~25 | £1.5M–£4M+ | Coastal, exclusive, cliff-side |

### Due Diligence Specific to Villas

1. **Cliff / slope stability** (Eastside, parts of South District)
   - **Geotechnical report essential** — not optional
   - Check: Retaining wall condition, drainage, monitoring history
   - Insurance: Specialist market; higher premiums; exclusions possible

2. **Plot boundaries & rights**
   - Full boundary walk with title plan
   - Rights of way (neighbour access, utility easements)
   - Foreshore / Crown land adjacency (Eastside)

3. **Services & infrastructure**
   - Private drainage / septic tank? (rare but exists)
   - Water pressure (elevated sites)
   - Electricity supply capacity (pool, EV charging, AC)

4. **Pool & outdoor amenities**
   - Pool condition, plant room, leaks history
   - Planning consent for pool/terrace/outbuilding
   - Maintenance costs (£3K–£6K/year typical)

5. **Security & privacy**
   - Boundary treatment (walls, hedges, CCTV)
   - Overlooking from higher ground (South District slopes)

## House vs Apartment: Cost of Ownership Comparison

| Annual Cost | 3-Bed Townhouse (Upper Town) | 3-Bed Apartment (Ocean Village) |
|-------------|------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Service charge / estate charge | £500–£1,500 | £4,000–£8,000 |
| Buildings insurance | £800–£1,500 | Included in service charge |
| Maintenance reserve (suggested) | £3,000–£5,000 | Covered by sinking fund |
| Ground rent (if leasehold) | £50–£300 | £100–£500 |
| **Total estimated annual** | **£4,350–£8,300** | **£4,100–£8,500** |

**Key insight**: Houses avoid high service charges but require **self-managed maintenance reserves**. A major repair (roof, damp, structural) on a house can cost £20K–£100K+ with no sinking fund to draw on.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I convert a townhouse into two flats?
Possible but complex: Planning consent, Building Control, freeholder consent (if leasehold), separate utilities, fire separation, separate Land Registry titles. Typically 12–24 months and £80K–£150K costs.

### What's the difference between a "house" and a "townhouse" in Gibraltar listings?
Agents use them interchangeably. "Townhouse" usually implies 3+ storeys on small footprint. "House" can mean terrace, semi-detached or detached. Always clarify: floors, footprint, garden, parking, tenure.

### Are there any new-build houses?
Very few. Occasional infill in South District (1–2 units/year). No volume house builders. New-build = apartments.

### Can I extend a terrace house sideways?
Usually no — party wall boundary. Rear extensions possible (subject to Planning, 3–4m depth typically). Roof extensions possible with Planning.

### How do I check if a property is freehold or leasehold?
Ask agent for **tenure confirmation in writing**. Your solicitor will obtain **Office Copy Entries** from Land Registry showing title class (Freehold / Leasehold) and lease details.

### What are the Stamp Duty implications for houses?
Same scale as apartments. First-time buyer relief (resident) on first £260,000. Higher rates for non-residents and companies.

## Conclusion

Gibraltar houses offer space, privacy and renovation upside that apartments cannot — but they demand **deeper due diligence** on structure, boundaries, leasehold terms and self-managed maintenance. The buyer who commissions a thorough RICS Level 3 survey, investigates leasehold terms early, and budgets realistically for maintenance will avoid the costly surprises that catch out apartment buyers used to service-charge-covered repairs.

**Priority order for house buyers**:
1. Leasehold term & freeholder consents
2. Structural survey (Level 3)
3. Boundary & drainage verification
4. Planning history & consents
5. Insurance quotation pre-offer

*This guide is general information. Always instruct a Gibraltar-qualified solicitor and RICS surveyor for specific properties.*
