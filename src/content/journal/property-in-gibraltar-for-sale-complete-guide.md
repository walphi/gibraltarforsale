# Property in Gibraltar for Sale: Complete Buyer's Guide 2027

Buying property in Gibraltar involves a distinct legal framework, eligibility rules and cost structure. This guide walks through the entire process — from establishing what you can buy to receiving keys — with practical checklists at each stage.

## 1. Establish Your Eligibility Before You Search

Gibraltar classifies residential property into categories that determine who can buy:

| Category | Who Can Buy | Typical Properties |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Open Market** | Anyone (Gibraltarians, UK nationals, EU/EEA, other foreign nationals) | Most private apartments, houses, new builds |
| **Restricted / Affordable Housing** | Eligible Gibraltarians only (residency + income criteria) | Government-sold estates (e.g., Mid-Harbour, Bayview Terraces) |
| **Crown Leasehold** | Varies by lease terms; often open market but with consent requirements | Many Town Area, Upper Town, some South District properties |

**Action**: Ask the agent or your solicitor to confirm the property's category **before** viewing. Do not rely on portal filters alone.

## 2. Calculate Your Total Budget (Not Just the Price)

| Cost Item | Typical Range | When Paid |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Purchase price** | Per property | Completion |
| **Stamp Duty** | See table below | Completion |
| **Legal fees (solicitor)** | £1,500–£3,500 + VAT | On account / completion |
| **Mortgage arrangement fee** | 0.5–1% of loan | Application / completion |
| **Valuation (bank)** | £300–£600 | Pre-offer |
| **Survey (RICS Level 2/3)** | £500–£1,500 | Pre-offer |
| **Land Registry / registration** | £100–£500 | Post-completion |
| **Service charge arrears (if any)** | Per development | Completion |
| **Removals / setup** | Variable | Moving day |

### Gibraltar Stamp Duty (2026/27 rates)

| Buyer Type | First £260,000 | £260,001–£350,000 | £350,001+ |
|------------|----------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **First-time buyer (resident)** | 0% | 2% | 5.5% |
| **Other resident** | 2% | 5.5% | 10% |
| **Non-resident** | 5% | 7.5% | 10% |

*First-time buyer relief requires Gibraltar residency and no prior property interest worldwide. Confirm with your solicitor.*

## 3. Secure Mortgage Agreement in Principle Early

Gibraltar lenders include: **Gibraltar International Bank, Trusted Novus Bank, Jyske Bank, NatWest International, Lloyds Bank Corporate Markets**.

| Lender | Max LTV (Resident) | Max LTV (Non-Resident) | Rate Type |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|-----------|
| Gibraltar International | 75% | 65% | Fixed / Tracker |
| Trusted Novus | 70% | 60% | Fixed |
| Jyske Bank | 75% | 65% | Tracker / Fixed |
| NatWest International | 70% | 60% | Fixed |

**Key constraints**:
- Affordability assessed on **net income after tax**; typically 4–4.5x income
- Non-residents need **30–40% deposit** and proof of overseas income
- Self-employed: 2–3 years accounts required
- Property must meet lender's valuation and suitability criteria (some exclude high service charge developments, short leaseholds, or certain new-build schemes)

## 4. The Purchase Timeline: Offer to Completion

```
Week 1-2:    Offer accepted → Memorandum of Sale issued
Week 2-3:    Instruct solicitor → Pay search fees → Mortgage application
Week 3-6:    Searches returned → Enquiries raised → Survey completed
Week 6-8:    Mortgage offer received → Contract drafted → Enquiries satisfied
Week 8-10:   Exchange of contracts → Deposit paid (10%) → Completion date set
Week 10-12:  Completion → Balance paid → Keys released → Registration
```

**Typical total: 10–14 weeks**. Leasehold properties with freeholder consent requirements can add 4–8 weeks.

## 5. Due Diligence: What Your Solicitor Must Check

| Search / Check | Purpose | Typical Cost |
|----------------|---------|--------------|
| **Land Registry title** | Confirm ownership, charges, restrictions | £50–£150 |
| **Lease & variations** | Term, ground rent, covenants, consents | Included in legal fee |
| **Service charge accounts** | 3 years' accounts; sinking fund; planned works | Included |
| **Management company** | Accounts, insurance, disputes | Included |
| **Planning & building control** | Permissions for alterations; enforcement notices | £100–£200 |
| **Local authority** | Road adoption, drainage, environmental | £150–£300 |
| **Chancel repair / manorial rights** | Rare but possible in older Town Area properties | £50 |

**Red flags** requiring specialist advice:
- Lease < 80 years unexpired
- Ground rent doubling clauses
- Service charge disputes or litigation
- Unauthorised alterations
- Flying freehold / shared access issues

## 6. Property Type-Specific Guidance

### Apartments (most common)
- Check **service charge history** (3+ years) and sinking fund balance
- Confirm **lift maintenance** contract and remaining life
- Verify **parking allocation** (deeded vs licence)
- Ask about **cladding / fire safety** remediation status

### Houses / Townhouses
- **Structural survey essential** (RICS Level 3)
- Check **party wall** agreements and neighbour relations
- Verify **boundary** positions against title plan
- Drainage CCTV recommended for older properties

### New Builds / Off-Plan
- **Developer track record** — check previous phases
- **Contract** — ensure long-stop date, sunset clause, snagging process
- **Warranties** — NHBC equivalent (Premier Guarantee, LABC) or architect's certificate
- **Service charge budget** — developer's estimate vs likely actuals

## 7. Completion Day & Post-Completion

**On completion day**:
1. Solicitor sends completion funds
2. Seller's solicitor confirms receipt → authorises key release
3. Keys collected from agent (usually 12–2pm)
4. Buyer's solicitor submits Land Registry application

**Post-completion (buyer's solicitor handles)**:
- Stamp Duty return & payment (within 30 days)
- Land Registry registration (2–8 weeks)
- Notify utility companies, service charge manager, Gibraltar Revenue Authority

## 8. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

| Pitfall | Prevention |
|---------|------------|
| Assuming UK process applies | Instruct a **Gibraltar-qualified solicitor** |
| Skipping survey on "modern" apartment | Hidden defects exist in all ages; budget £500–£1,500 |
| Ignoring leasehold terms | Short leases destroy value; check enfranchisement |
| Not budgeting for service charges | Can exceed £500/month in marina developments |
| Verbal assurances on view/parking | Get written confirmation; check title plan |
| Rushing exchange without mortgage offer | Mortgage offer expiry must align with completion date |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I buy through a company?
Yes, but Stamp Duty is at non-resident rates (higher). Company ownership has tax implications in your home jurisdiction. Seek cross-border tax advice.

### What if I'm buying with a partner?
Joint tenancy vs tenancy in common affects succession and tax. Discuss with solicitor before exchange.

### Can I rent out the property?
Open market: generally yes (check lease). Affordable housing: restrictions apply. Short-term lets may require Tourism Licence and Planning consent.

### What currency is used?
Prices in GBP. Mortgages can be GBP or EUR. Exchange rate risk exists for EUR income / GBP mortgage.

### Do I need to be in Gibraltar to complete?
No — completion by correspondence is standard. Power of attorney to your solicitor handles signing.

## Conclusion

Gibraltar property purchase is straightforward **when you have the right team**: a Gibraltar solicitor, a lender familiar with local stock, and a RICS surveyor. The legal framework protects buyers but requires local knowledge to navigate leasehold, service charges and category restrictions.

Start by confirming your eligibility category, securing an Agreement in Principle, and instructing a solicitor **before** you make an offer.
