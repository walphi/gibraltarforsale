# Eastside Property for Sale

## Eastside at a glance

Eastside is Gibraltar’s Mediterranean-facing shoreline beneath the Rock. Its best-known localities include Eastern Beach, Catalan Bay and Sandy Bay. Rather than one continuous town centre, it is a sequence of settings: open sand and a busy road corridor at one end, a sheltered cove and village identity at another. “Eastside” property searches can refer to established residential blocks around Devil’s Tower Road as well as homes closer to the beaches, so pin down each building and address before comparing properties.

The Government’s visitor guide describes Catalan Bay as an old fishing village formed by Genoese settlers, with rocky slopes, a sheltered cove, fish restaurants and the Church of Our Lady of Sorrows. This gives the bay a different feel from the more open shoreline at Eastern Beach. It is a general description, not a guarantee that a particular business is open or a property has a sea view. [1]

## Homes and property types

Established housing is mainly apartment-led around the Devil’s Tower Road corridor. Government information records completed housing at Beach View Terraces, Mons Calpe Mews and Hassan Centenary Terraces. These are useful search anchors, but an area label does not confirm an individual property’s tenure, eligibility, title, condition or resale restrictions. [2]

Near Catalan Bay, the setting is more village-like, with the beach and older settlement close together. Buyers should inspect the immediate surroundings rather than assume the locality consists only of traditional houses. For apartments, compare floor level, lift access, orientation, outlook, natural light, storage and parking. For homes near the shore, ask about upkeep of exposed windows and external finishes, and review building records rather than relying on a viewing alone.

Eastside also has a separate planned redevelopment site. In April 2025, the Development and Planning Commission granted full planning permission for the first phase, concerning coastal protection works and a marina. The Government’s environmental-statement page describes a broader mixed-use proposal that includes homes, hotels, commercial space and public realm. Permission and proposal descriptions do not mean future homes or facilities are complete, available to buy or due on a particular timetable. Check current planning records and works status before treating the project as part of the neighbourhood today. [5] [6]

## Coast, transport and everyday facilities

The three Eastside beaches define the area. Catalan Bay’s cove and village character contrasts with the broader beach setting at Eastern Beach, with Sandy Bay further along the eastern shore. Government visitor material describes the eastern side as a route for walking or travelling by bus between the beaches. Comfort and journey time depend on weather, route and mobility, so check conditions before relying on it for a daily commute. [1]

Public transport is an option, but confirm the nearest stop and current timetable for each address. Citibus lists Devil’s Tower Road stops serving Beach View Terraces and names Eastern Beach and Catalan Bay; the wider route extends to airport, frontier and city-centre stops. The route page helps with orientation but is not a guarantee of frequency or journey time. [3] A separate Government Beach Bus served Eastside and Southside beaches seasonally in 2026, from 6 July to 30 August; that dated service is not year-round transport. [4]

Day-to-day convenience depends on which end of Eastside you choose. The main road corridor brings bus stops and access toward the airport and frontier, while Catalan Bay offers a beachside village setting. For broader shopping, work and services, check door-to-door journeys into the city centre rather than assuming every facility is within an easy walk. Verify nearby food options, healthcare, schools and beach services against current information; this guide does not promise that a named facility is open year-round.

## Is Eastside a good fit?

Eastside may suit buyers who value proximity to the sea, prefer to be outside the central commercial streets or like Catalan Bay’s cove setting. Bus links offer an alternative to driving and connect toward Gibraltar’s airport, frontier and centre. [3]

The trade-offs are address-specific. A beach-facing position can mean exposure to wind, salt and seasonal visitors. Devil’s Tower Road is a through-route, so assess traffic noise, crossings, parking and your regular walking route at the times you would use them. Ask whether nearby works could affect outlook or access, and verify construction information directly. The planned Eastside project is distinct from completed homes in existing developments. A coastal address does not guarantee a sandy beach at the door, a clear view or a step-free route.

## Nearby areas

Within Eastside, compare Devil’s Tower Road residential blocks with Catalan Bay and the stretch toward Sandy Bay. The airport and frontier are toward the northern end of the transport corridor, while bus routes also continue toward the city centre. If weighing Eastside against a central address, compare real journeys and facilities rather than map distance alone. [3]
