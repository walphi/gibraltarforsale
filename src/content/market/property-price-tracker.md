# Gibraltar Property Price Tracker

## Current data status

This tracker is being established with a reliable, current dataset. No verified listing feed or private residential sale-price dataset has been published yet. It therefore reports no median price, price per square metre, transaction count, time to sell, rental yield or market trend. A blank result is more useful than a precise-looking number built from incomplete records.

The Government Statistics Office describes its role as collecting and publishing statistics on areas including employment, population, tourism, trade and retail prices.[1] Its 2019 archive has a “Property Sales” category, but the listed measures concern sales crediting the Improvement and Development Fund and government-owned property companies—not a current, comprehensive series of private home sales.[2] The government’s housing topic page lists waiting lists, rent relief and government rental-home arrears, not a private-market sale-price index.[3] These pages show why this tracker needs a documented, current dataset before reporting market results.

## What the tracker would measure

The proposed tracker would keep asking prices and achieved sale prices separate. Asking prices describe properties advertised during a period. Achieved prices describe completed transactions with price and date verified against a dependable source. A listing is not a sale, and an advertised price is not evidence of the amount paid.

For each series, the main summary would be the median—the middle observation when values are ordered—alongside the record count and period. A median is less affected by an unusually high or low value than an average, but it can still shift when the mix of properties changes. A period with more small flats, for example, is not directly comparable to one with more large homes. Breakdowns by property type, bedroom band or location should appear only where records support fair comparisons. A £/sqm measure would require a consistent, verified internal floor area; omit it where area is missing or incomparable.

Other measures require their own evidence. Days on market needs dated listing histories and a consistent definition of when marketing starts and ends. Time from first advert to completion is not the same as time to agree a sale. Rental yield requires representative rents matched to comparable sale values; it should not be inferred from a few advertised rents and asking prices.

## Proposed data and quality rules

A release should name its listing and transaction sources, explain the coverage period, and disclose where records are incomplete. Possible inputs include agency feeds, owner listings where permitted, and official transaction records if accessible and suitable. Permission, coverage and definitions must be checked before use. Gibraltar’s Land Titles Act concerns registration of deeds and wills in the Land Titles Register; a registry record should not automatically be treated as a clean statistical transaction row.[4]

To reduce duplicates, records would be matched using normalized address or unit identifiers, listing references, property attributes, price and dates. Relisted adverts and updates should count once in a listing snapshot; genuine price changes should be kept as history, not new homes. Possible duplicate sales, non-market transfers, unclear property types and incomplete or implausible values should be reviewed or excluded under published rules. Coverage can vary by provider, area and property type, so the tracker must not imply it includes every home or sale unless that is demonstrated.

As a proposal, use calendar quarters for headline periods, refresh quarterly after checking late records, and show the usable sample size beside each result. Suppress segments with fewer than 10 observations; a rolling 12-month period may be used when a quarterly sample is too small, but neither approach guarantees representativeness. Fix and document these rules in advance. If verification or source coverage is inadequate, report “insufficient verified data” rather than a stale result.

## How to use it

The tracker would describe its records, not value an individual home or predict a sale price. Ask whether the figure is advertised or achieved, what dates define the period, how many records support it, and whether the property mix is comparable. A small or changing sample can move sharply without a broad market shift. For a particular home, also consider condition, tenure, service charges and comparable evidence.

**Buyer questions—not tracker findings:** What evidence supports the asking price? Are floor area, storage and parking described consistently? Is the home vacant, tenanted or subject to other arrangements? What checks, permissions, financing and professional fees apply to this purchase? Confirm current answers with relevant Gibraltar authorities and qualified local professionals.

## FAQs

### Does this page show current Gibraltar prices?

Not yet. A reliable, current dataset has not been published, so there are no prices, counts, yields or trends to report. Register at <a href="/contact/" class="font-bold text-sea-700 underline">Contact</a> for launch notifications.

### Why might asking and achieved prices differ?

They describe different stages. An advert gives the seller’s stated price; an achieved-price series needs evidence of a completed transaction and consistent price and date definitions. One cannot stand in for the other.
