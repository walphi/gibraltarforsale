# Gibraltar Residency and Property

A home in Gibraltar can be an important part of a move, but owning one and having permission to live or work in Gibraltar are different matters. The Government’s residence process requires applicants to meet eligibility rules and receive a residence permit; property ownership is one possible way to evidence accommodation for certain applicants, not a route that itself grants immigration status. [1] [2]

Before making an offer, establish which rules apply to you. Nationality, existing documents, employment, family ties and any earlier application can affect the answer. Treat property, residence, work permission and future status applications separately.

## Four things to keep separate

**Property ownership** concerns your rights in a home. Buying a home does not, by itself, give permission to enter, reside or work in Gibraltar. The Government’s Residency Portal assesses residence applications under the statutory framework and separately points to immigration, employment and business processes. [1] [2]

**Permission to reside** is an immigration decision. Applicants use the official platform and must satisfy requirements for their route. The employee criteria include an eligible contract, accommodation, age and vetting requirements; a work permit is needed for some. Family members, students and self-employed people may follow different processes. [3]

**Immigration status** is not the same as having a deed, rental agreement, tax arrangement or a residence card issued for another purpose. Residence permission is granted and administered by the Department of Immigration and Home Affairs. Existing cardholders or people with established rights may be treated differently from new applicants; check the Government’s transition guidance rather than assuming today’s criteria apply identically to everyone. [1] [4]

**Permission to work** is separate. Where required, a work permit is handled by the Department of Employment. The employer must obtain approval in principle before the residence application proceeds, and the permit and residence card must be issued before work starts. British, EU, EEA and Swiss nationals do not need a work permit under current guidance, but employment alone does not give the right to live in Gibraltar. [5]

## How a home fits into an application

For an employee applying through the standard route, the official checklist asks for evidence that they rent or own a Gibraltar property. A rental must be the applicant’s main home for at least 12 months and not a holiday let. An owned property must be available for the applicant’s exclusive use and cannot be let while they hold the residence permit. These are conditions of that residence route; do not assume they apply in the same way to every category or that satisfying them guarantees approval. [3]

The Residency Regulations 2026 came into operation on 14 July 2026. The portal says the new criteria apply to applications made on or after 6 October 2025; some people who held a valid Civilian Registration Card or Permit of Residence on that date remain under the previous regime. Confirm your position against official guidance because transitional rights are fact-specific. [1] [2] [4]

## A practical order of checks

1. **Identify your route and status.** Read the [official eligibility guidance](https://residency.gov.gi/residency/check-if-you-can-apply/) and transition notes. If you already have a Gibraltar document or lived there previously, confirm whether the new criteria cover you.
2. **Check work permission.** Ask the employer whether a work permit and prior approval are required. Do not confuse permission to work with permission to reside. [5]
3. **Check the accommodation test.** Confirm exclusive use if buying, or primary-home and minimum-term requirements if renting. Have local advisers check title, use, occupancy and transaction details.
4. **Use the government process.** Buying is not applying or receiving approval. Wait for the decision and required permit before treating residence or work as authorised. Applications undergo eligibility, identity, security and due-diligence checks. [2]

## Common questions

### If I buy a property, can I move to Gibraltar?
Not on that fact alone. Buying a home does not automatically confer a residence permit, immigration status or right to work. Check the rules for your own circumstances before committing to a purchase. [1] [3] [5]

### Does owning a home help with a residence application?
It may satisfy the accommodation-evidence part of the standard employee route if the official conditions are met. The rest of the application still needs to qualify, and an approval is not guaranteed. Other routes may differ. [3]

### Does a residence permit make me a Gibraltarian or give every public benefit?
No. Residence permission and Gibraltarian Status are separate. Eligibility for the latter depends on the person’s circumstances and period of lawful residence. [1] [4]

## Important note

This guide is general information, not legal, tax, immigration, mortgage or property advice. Rules can change, and individual circumstances matter. Confirm current requirements with Gibraltar Government services and obtain advice from appropriately qualified local professionals before buying, relocating, applying for residence or starting work.
