# Gibraltar Property Viewing Checklist

A viewing is a short inspection, not proof that a home is sound or its paperwork is in order. Use this checklist to record what you see, then list documents and professional checks to complete before committing. Visit at a different time if noise, light or traffic may affect daily life. Browse [property for sale](/property-for-sale/) or compare neighbourhood context through [Gibraltar areas](/areas/).

Record the date, time, weather, address, floor and agent’s answers. Photograph details with permission. Mark **Yes / No / Unsure** and note who will follow up.

## 1. Visible checks during the viewing

### Condition, damp and ventilation

- [ ] Look at ceilings and walls for stains, bubbling paint, cracks, mould, fresh patches or unusual odours. Note locations; a clean surface does not prove a damp issue is resolved.
- [ ] Check windows, external walls, bathrooms, kitchen and under sinks for condensation, soft timber, damaged sealant, leaks or corrosion.
- [ ] Open windows and doors if permitted. Check locks, airflow and extractor fans; note stuffiness. Do not dismantle fittings or move heavy items.
- [ ] Run taps and showers briefly with permission. Check pressure, drainage, hot water, toilet flushing and visible leaks.
- [ ] Inspect floors, tiles, cupboards, visible wiring and sockets. A viewing is not a structural, electrical or plumbing inspection.

### Light, noise and shared access

- [ ] Note daylight by room and time, outlook, shading and privacy. Ask separately whether nearby development could change the outlook.
- [ ] Pause and listen with windows open and closed. Note traffic, neighbours, lifts, plant rooms, venues and construction. One visit cannot represent every day.
- [ ] Walk from entrance to the flat. Check shared doors, lighting, cleanliness, stairs, handrails and lift. Ask about step-free access and lift outages.
- [ ] If parking or storage is offered, identify the exact space and check access, dimensions and use restrictions. Do not assume it is included because it is nearby or pictured.

### Utilities and daily use

- [ ] Identify visible meters if accessible. Ask which serve the property, whether supplies are active and whether reconnection or inspection is needed.
- [ ] Check mobile signal, internet options, ventilation, heating or cooling, water-heating controls and included appliances.
- [ ] Consider deliveries, refuse, storage, accessibility, pets and visitor access.

## 2. Ask the agent or seller; verify on paper

Record answers and request supporting documents; do not rely only on a verbal response.

- [ ] **Tenure and title:** What exactly is being sold? Ask about the term, remaining lease, renewal provisions, restrictions, rights and obligations. Have a Gibraltar conveyancer review title and deeds or lease. Gibraltar has a statutory land-title framework, and Land Property Services lists land registration among its services; neither replaces a property-specific legal check. [1] [2]
- [ ] **Alterations and completion:** Ask what extensions or other works were carried out, and request approvals, plans and any completion or fitness documents that apply. Gibraltar’s Town Planning page says development requires planning permission, with building-regulation approval where relevant. Building Control describes inspection against approved specifications and a Certificate of Fitness process for satisfactory new buildings. Ask a conveyancer or surveyor what applies to this home. [3] [4]
- [ ] **Service or community charges:** Request the current budget, recent statements, coverage, payment frequency, arrears status, reserve funds and proposed extra contributions. Confirm the amount and obligations for this unit. LPS lists service-charge information among its public services, but that does not verify a particular property's charges. [2]
- [ ] **Building works and management:** Request meeting minutes, maintenance plans, inspection reports, insurance details and information on known defects, leaks, lift servicing or proposed major works. Ask who manages repairs and shared access, how issues are reported, and whether rules affect your intended use.
- [ ] **Parking and utilities:** Confirm any parking or storage rights in writing. Request recent utility bills, meter/account details and supply status. GEA publishes electricity tariffs and charges; AquaGib provides applications and meter-reading functions. Verify current rates, account arrangements and reconnection needs for the specific home. [5] [6]
- [ ] **Nearby development:** Ask about known proposed or approved works. Check relevant planning information or seek professional advice if the effect matters; the official planning department describes its role in development control. [3]

## 3. Before deciding

Separate confirmed facts from unanswered questions. Ask the agent for missing records in writing. Arrange an independent survey suited to the home, and instruct a Gibraltar conveyancer to check title, tenure, permissions, charges, restrictions and transaction requirements. Obtain mortgage, tax and residency advice from qualified professionals before relying on assumptions.

Use [property for sale](/property-for-sale/) and [areas](/areas/) to explore options and location needs, not to infer a particular home’s condition or future value.

## FAQs

### Is one viewing enough?

A second visit at another time may reveal different light, noise or access conditions. A survey and document review answer different questions from a walk-through.

### Should I accept an agent’s answer about permissions or charges?

Treat it as a lead, not verification. Request the records and ask the appropriate professional to confirm their meaning and relevance to this property.

### Is this legal or financial advice?

No. This checklist is general information, not legal, tax, residency or mortgage advice. Documents and requirements vary. Consult qualified professionals before deciding.
