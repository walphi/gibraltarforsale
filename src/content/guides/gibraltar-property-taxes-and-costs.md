# Gibraltar Property Taxes and Costs

The advertised price is only one part of a Gibraltar purchase budget. Stamp duty depends on the property's value, its use and whether each purchaser meets the statutory first- or second-time-buyer test. A mortgage may bring separate duty, while legal, lender and building costs depend on the transaction. Use this page as a checklist, not a quote: ask a Gibraltar conveyancing lawyer and Land Property Services (LPS) to confirm figures for the actual property and buyers. You can [browse homes for sale](/property-for-sale/) and explore the [area guides](/areas/) as you compare options.

## Property transfer stamp duty

Stamp duty applies to a conveyance or transfer of Gibraltar real property. The statutory schedule makes the purchaser or transferee liable on a sale. The rates below are marginal, meaning different portions of the consideration are charged at different rates. The Act also refers to the value of the whole property, so ask LPS to confirm the valuation and calculation basis before relying on an estimate. [1] [2]

For a purchase outside the special residential-buyer schedule, the published bands are:

- Whole-property value of £200,000 or less: 0%.
- Above £200,000 and up to £350,000: 2% on the first £250,000, then 5.5% on the amount above £250,000.
- Above £350,000: 3% on the first £350,000, 3.5% on the portion above £350,000 up to £800,000, and 4.5% on the amount above £800,000. [1] [2]

A separate schedule applies if each purchaser is a first- or second-time buyer and the property is entirely residential for use as one dwelling. It sets 0% on the first £300,000, 5.5% on the portion from £300,000 to £350,000, and 3.5% above £350,000 up to £800,000. The 2024 Budget describes the benefit as owner-occupier relief restricted to properties under £800,000. The amended Act's schedule also prints a rate for amounts above £800,000 in this category. Because those sources leave a practical eligibility question for a higher-value purchase, confirm the current treatment with LPS or the Commissioner of Stamp Duties before budgeting on relief. [1] [2] [3]

“First home” alone does not establish eligibility: the test concerns each purchaser and the dwelling's use. Joint or company purchases, mixed use, multiple units and values near thresholds need case-specific advice. LPS offers a Stamp Duty Calculator, but verify the inputs and assessment. [4]

## Mortgage duty and other costs

The statutory mortgage schedule specifies 0.13% for each £100 or part of the secured amount up to £200,000, and 0.20% where the amount exceeds £200,000. It identifies the mortgagee (lender) as liable for this duty. Whether a related cost is passed to the borrower depends on the loan terms, so ask the lender for a written itemisation. [1]

Keep non-tax costs separate. Ask your lawyer to quote for conveyancing and title checks, and ask LPS which stamping, registration, certificate or search charges apply. LPS confirms it handles land-registration and stamp-duty services, but there is no single total fee verified here for every transfer. Also request lender and valuation charges, and check the lease or management documents for service charges, insurance contributions, planned works and sums apportioned at completion. These vary by property and transaction, so avoid generic percentage estimates. [4] [5]

The 7.5% special stamp duty is not a general buyer surcharge. The Act applies it to qualifying resales at Beach View Terraces and Mons Calpe Mews during the specified ten-year period. The seller or transferor pays it in addition to ordinary buyer duty. The Land Management Committee may direct exceptions for specified forced sales or meritorious upgrades. If a property is in either estate, have your lawyer check the underlease, timing and required certificate before exchange. [1] [6]

For off-plan purchases, do not include the often-mentioned 0.5% assignment duty as an established current charge without checking. A 2025 parliamentary Bill proposed a special duty on off-plan agreements and assignments, but PwC's August 2026 review said it had not been enacted. Ask LPS or your lawyer to confirm the law applicable to the instrument and completion date. [7] [8]

## Before you commit

1. Ask your lawyer to identify the transaction type and calculate duty, including any buyer relief.
2. Give LPS the property's full value, intended use, purchaser details and information about any assignment or mortgage. Request confirmation of the assessment and registration charges.
3. Get written estimates for legal work, lender fees and valuation. Review the title, lease, service-charge accounts and completion apportionments.
4. If you plan to rent the property, account for income tax and compliance. Gibraltar's Income Tax Office says rental income from Gibraltar property is taxable and requires registration and annual returns. Ask a tax adviser how your circumstances and ownership structure affect the result. [9]

## FAQs

### Is stamp duty an annual property tax?

No. Stamp duty is a transaction duty. LPS separately handles rating assessments and valuations for rates purposes. Ask about the property's rates position and any charges apportioned at completion. [4] [5]

### Does every buyer pay the same rate?

No. The bands depend on whole-property value and transaction circumstances. Residential first- and second-time-buyer treatment has additional conditions. Confirm eligibility rather than relying on a portal estimate alone. [1] [2] [3]

### Who can confirm the exact amount?

A Gibraltar-qualified conveyancing lawyer can advise on the transaction. LPS handles stamp-duty and land-registration services, while a tax adviser can assess tax or residency questions. Documents, lender terms and property status affect the final figure.

## Important disclaimer

This guide is general information, not legal, tax, residency, mortgage or financial advice. It does not determine eligibility or assess a particular property. Rules, administrative practice and documents may change. Obtain advice from qualified local professionals before making an offer, signing an agreement or relying on a tax calculation.
