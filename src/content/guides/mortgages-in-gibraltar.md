# Mortgages in Gibraltar

A mortgage conversation is easier when you know what to establish: how much you can contribute, what evidence a lender may request, and whether your circumstances fit its product. There is no single checklist or eligibility test that applies to every Gibraltar lender. Treat an early estimate as provisional and get lender-specific confirmation before committing to a purchase.

Compare [properties for sale](/property-for-sale/) and explore [Gibraltar’s areas](/areas/), but keep your search budget separate from a lender’s final decision. An indicative borrowing figure is not an offer or guarantee of approval.

## What a lender may want to understand

A lender needs enough information to assess the borrowing and verify your details. Gibraltar International Bank’s public checklist, for example, asks for identification and address evidence, income records and bank statements; it lists further documents for self-employed applicants, business owners and buy-to-let cases.[1] Its first-time-buyer page has a different product-specific list, including income evidence, property details, bank statements and proof of savings.[2] These examples are not universal rules. Ask your chosen lender for its current list.

Income may come from employment, self-employment, a business or rent. Ask which evidence it accepts, how it treats variable pay or rental income, and whether it needs employment records, accounts or tax documents. If income is paid in a currency different from the mortgage, ask how currency conversion and exchange-rate changes are considered. These are questions to clarify, not assumptions about a lender’s policy.

Residency and employment can matter differently across products. Gibraltar International Bank states Gibraltar residency and a Gibraltar-based income source for its published first-time-buyer mortgage.[2] That is a condition for that product, not a jurisdiction-wide rule. Tell the lender where you live and work, who employs you, and where you are paid; ask whether those details affect the product you are considering.

## Deposit, equity and the property

Your deposit is the amount you plan to contribute rather than borrow. If you already own a property, equity is the value left after relevant borrowing is accounted for; ask how the lender would establish any usable equity. A lender may ask you to show where your contribution came from. Gibraltar International Bank, for example, includes statements for savings or funds intended as the property contribution in its checklist.[1] The amount required, accepted source of funds and any product limits must be confirmed directly. Do not rely on a generic percentage or another buyer’s experience.

Ask how the property is assessed, what valuation or other checks are needed, and whether the lender’s valuation differs from your own survey. Confirm what happens if its valuation is below the agreed price. Keep a separate allowance for purchase and professional costs, and ask a Gibraltar conveyancing professional which costs and taxes apply to your transaction; this guide does not set them out.

## A practical application checklist

1. **Set a comfortable ceiling.** Work out what monthly payment and upfront contribution fit your budget. Ask how the lender reaches any borrowing estimate and what could change it.
2. **Request the current document list.** Clarify requirements for your income, savings, debts and the property. Ask whether documents must be recent, translated or certified.
3. **Explain cross-border details early.** Discuss residence, employer location, income currency and overseas accounts or commitments. Ask how each affects the application.
4. **Understand the written offer.** Check repayment basis, rate structure, term, fees, conditions, early-repayment provisions and offer expiry directly with the lender. Details vary; do not assume a quote is final or currently available.
5. **Keep contingencies clear.** Before a binding commitment, ask your solicitor and lender what milestones remain and what happens if approval or valuation is delayed.

## Questions to ask a broker or lender

- Do you advise across the market, or arrange only your institution’s products? How are you paid, and what fees would I owe?
- How do you assess my income type, regular commitments, deposit source and any currency differences?
- Do residency, employer location or employment status affect this particular application?
- What valuation, legal and other checks are required, and which costs are outside the mortgage?
- What conditions remain before funds can be released, and how long is any offer valid?

If using an adviser or firm, check the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission’s regulated-entities registers and confirm the relevant permission. The Commission says registers are updated regularly and advises consumers to contact it if a firm’s status is unclear.[3] A listing alone does not show whether a service is suitable for you.

## FAQs

### Can I find out how much I can borrow before choosing a property?
Ask for an initial discussion or indication, but it is not a final lending decision. Ask what assumptions and checks remain.

### Is a stated deposit percentage a Gibraltar-wide rule?
This guide cannot verify a percentage that applies to all buyers and lenders. Ask about the product and how your contribution will be verified.

### Does living or working in Gibraltar guarantee approval?
No. Requirements depend on lender and product, and one condition does not guarantee approval. Confirm your circumstances directly.

### Is this legal, tax or financial advice?
No. This is general information, not professional mortgage, legal, tax or residency advice. Rules and product terms can change. Obtain current advice from an appropriately authorised mortgage professional, a Gibraltar lawyer and a tax adviser as relevant before acting.
