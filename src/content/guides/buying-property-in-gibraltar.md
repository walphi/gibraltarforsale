# Buying Property in Gibraltar

Treat each viewing as the start of a decision, not a commitment. Check that the property fits your budget and intended use, then let a Gibraltar solicitor examine the documents before you sign or transfer money. Browse [property for sale](/property-for-sale/) and compare locations in our [Gibraltar areas guide](/areas/).

## 1. Plan and confirm what you can buy

Set a total budget, not just a price ceiling. Allow for legal and registration charges, any applicable stamp duty, mortgage and valuation costs, survey work, insurance and ongoing service charges or ground rent. The Stamp Duties Act sets out rules for property conveyances, but the amount depends on current law and the details of the purchase. Ask your solicitor to confirm the calculation; do not rely on an old estimate. [1]

Check the property category and conditions early. “Open Market” and “Restricted Market” are labels, not substitutes for checking the title, lease, buyer eligibility and consent requirements. Government guidance on certain older affordable-housing estates describes conditions for lifting some resale restrictions and says consent can still be required. Ask your solicitor and, where relevant, Land Property Services to confirm the exact position. [2]

If you need a mortgage, speak to a lender or qualified adviser early. An initial indication is not a final offer, and a lender may assess the property as well as your finances. Discuss joint ownership or a company or trust purchase with legal and tax advisers before offering.

## 2. View with a checklist

Look beyond the finish and record questions for your solicitor rather than relying on verbal assurances:

- Is it freehold or leasehold? If leasehold, what is the remaining term? What do the lease or underlease say about use, letting, alterations, repairs and consents?
- What are the service charges and ground rent? Are there arrears, planned works or reserve funds?
- Are parking, storage, terraces and access included as legal rights?
- Do alterations have required permissions and consents, and does the layout match the records?
- Would an independent survey or specialist inspection be sensible given the condition or proposed works?

Local due-diligence guidance highlights title, lease terms, building management, planning and physical condition as matters buyers may need to investigate. The checks vary by property. [3]

## 3. Make an offer without rushing paperwork

State the price and key assumptions clearly. These might include mortgage approval, an inspection, included fixtures or a preferred completion date. Ask whether a deadline or other condition is proposed.

Have your solicitor review any memorandum of sale or reservation agreement before signing or paying. “Reservation” alone does not establish whether money is refundable, what happens if checks reveal a problem, or whether you are committed. Confirm who holds the payment, what it secures, return conditions and deadlines. Reservation agreements are used in some Gibraltar conveyancing, including some off-plan transactions, but the document and circumstances matter. [4]

Be ready to provide identity and funding records if requested. Ask early what evidence is needed if funds come from several sources, a business, a gift or a sale of another asset.

## 4. Instruct a solicitor and check the property

Choose a Gibraltar solicitor acting for you; request a written estimate of work, fees and likely disbursements. Local residential conveyancing may involve title, contracts, leases, finance and registration, depending on the transaction. [4]

Ask what searches and documents are appropriate. These may cover Land Registry and title records, the seller’s authority, the lease and restrictions, mortgages or charges, service-charge information, planning or building-control records, and consents. Gibraltar’s Land Titles Act addresses registration of deeds and wills in the Land Titles Register. Ask your solicitor what a search establishes and what remains unchecked. [5]

Resolve material questions before signing the purchase contract. Request missing documents and discuss any unresolved issue with your solicitor. Legal checks do not replace a physical survey or your own assessment of noise, access, light and daily travel.

## 5. Understand signing, completion and handover

Do not assume an accepted offer, reservation, signature or payment has the same effect in every purchase. Ask your solicitor which document creates obligations, when you become bound, what deposit is due and what happens if either party cannot complete. There is no universal timeline or payment sequence without reviewing the documents.

Before completion, request a statement of the balance and costs, including applicable stamp duty and registration charges. Confirm mortgage conditions and consents, arrange timely funds, and record arrangements for keys and possession. After completion, ask what registration or filings remain and obtain copies of final documents.

If you will occupy the property, check current Government instructions for registering property occupation. Government guidance says the register applies to people residing in a property, whether owned, rented or licensed. [7]

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I buy before confirming eligibility?

Viewings are a chance to ask questions, but do not assume every property is available to every buyer. Confirm category, eligibility and consent requirements with a solicitor before signing or paying.

### Is a reservation payment the same as a contract deposit?

Not necessarily. The written terms determine what it does, who holds it and whether it can be returned. Ask your solicitor before paying.

### What does leasehold mean?

The property is held under a lease, not as freehold land. The term, permitted use, repair obligations and consents can affect ownership. Ask a Gibraltar solicitor to explain the lease.

### Is this legal or tax advice?

No. This is general information, not legal, tax, residency or mortgage advice. Obtain current, transaction-specific advice from a qualified Gibraltar solicitor and the relevant professional.
